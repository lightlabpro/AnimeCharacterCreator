# Mastering Fantasy Art: Drawing Dynamic Characters - John Stanko (417 pp) - reading notes (q20)

Read: full text layer (every page) + every page image (47 3x3 sheets). Realistic illustration from photo references:
Part I (p8-50) reference, lighting, models; Part II (p52-392) 16 demos; gallery (p393-410); back matter.

## Points that apply to our head
- "We all know what our mom looks like, but we can't draw her" (p6): draw from reference, not memory. This is the
  same lesson as Edwards' symbol system. For us: judge the head against the MHS3 crops, not from memory of anime.
- If the mouth looks off, compare with the reference to decide whether to move it up/down, make it bigger/smaller or
  change its values (the top lip darker, the bottom lip larger) (p12).
- Lighting (p35-47): a key light from an angle, NOT straight on - straight-on light flattens the planes and contours;
  a much softer fill opposite; even light from both sides makes a shadow line down the middle of the face; optional
  rim light. Good portrait light has directional shadows that unify the face shape (p45-47).
- Sorceress (p54-72): 3/4 head from slightly below; centre line first; eye/nose/mouth guidelines at about 15 deg in
  that view (they wrap and tilt with the head); block in eye, nose and mouth widths; a nose turned up too much looks
  piglike (p60); shadows are not one tone but vary - subtle value changes give depth (p64); the shadow right under the
  chin is much darker than the middle of the neck (p65); "Draw harder": a feature a few degrees off or a bit too high
  makes "something looks weird" - fix it, never "close enough" (p65-66).
- Barbarian (p73-92), shouting open mouth: "Don't just create one big black shape for the mouth. Take the time to draw
  in the teeth and tongue to add energy to the expression" (p83); in the image the teeth are separate shapes with a
  dark interior behind them, the tongue sits low, and the upper teeth catch light while the lower ones are shadowed by
  the lip.
- Vampire (p293-309): open snarl, the shape of the nose changes with the snarl; shade the planes of the nose (p301).
- Elf ear (p187-188): adjust the anatomy, do not just add a point to a human ear.
- Dwarf (p130): never draw a face from a memorized pattern (oval, dividing lines, almond eyes, triangle nose); that
  pattern only works for that one face from that one angle - break it into geometric forms to draw it from any angle.
- Photo lens distortion (p240-241): a near hand looks too big in a photo; draw from life to learn the difference.
  Relevant to our render camera: the app's perspective must not distort the face (done in q18).
- Overlap and value separation (p330), tangents (p363), draw large (p371).

## What this means for head_mhs3_n56 (q20)
- The open-mouth interior must not be one flat dark shape: teeth as lit shapes, tongue low, a darker throat behind.
  This is the q19 throat/teeth-shade layer direction - keep it and check it in the 3/4 render.
- Our light must stay off-axis (key from an angle) so the upper lip and nose planes read; check the app's default
  light is not straight on.
- Features must follow the turned head (guidelines tilt and wrap) - the frontal-almond eye at 3/4 is the
  "memorized pattern" failure.
