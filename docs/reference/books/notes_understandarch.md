# Understanding Architecture Through Drawing (2nd ed.) - Brian Edwards (Taylor & Francis 2008, 273 PDF pages) - reading notes (q20)

Read: full text layer (every page, 5 chunks) + every page image (8 combined sheets). An architecture sketching
and design-theory book; no figure or face content. Page numbers are PDF pages.

## Core content
- Why draw (p10-38): drawing as analysis rather than record; "It is better to capture the essence rather than seek
  an exhaustive realism. Designers need to know what to leave suggested" (p12); sketches edit out what a
  photograph keeps (p33); drawing as a language with shared conventions (p38).
- Choosing the subject (p39-44): sunlight at an angle, not straight on, brings out relief; the convention is a
  sun at 45 degrees over the left shoulder (p43, p55); shadows give solidity and show depth (p42).
- Perspective (p47-54): eye level and vanishing points; foreground lines thickest, darkest shadow and brightest
  color, distance the reverse (p51); distant windows become a single line (p53); diagonals for centres (p54).
- Line and shade (p55-58): shade the sides turned away from an (imagined) light; darker shade in the
  foreground, lighter going back (p57); stipple, hatch, tone variants of one drawing (p56).
- Composition (p59-68): subject in the middle third, golden section; layers of foreground/middle/background;
  leave large areas unrendered - "A common mistake is to draw to the same level of intensity right across the
  sheet" (p67); balance a dominant direction with counter-lines; triangular groupings.
- Practice, plans, sequential sketches, photography (p69-96); townscape case studies - squares, streets,
  landmarks, gateways, facades (proportion, structure, function), machinery, landscape, sustainability,
  archaeology, interiors, urban analysis (p99-204); sketchbook subjects, sketch to design, designing through
  drawing, interviews with ten UK architects on drawing vs model vs CAD (p207-268).

## What this means for head_mhs3_n56 (q20)
- No anatomy. Transferable: a key light about 45 degrees above and to one side (the classic "over the left
  shoulder" convention) is the light that best reveals relief - our upper lip and mouth interior only read with
  depth when the light rakes from above at an angle, not from straight in front (p42-43, p55). Board lighting
  should use it.
- Uneven intensity (p67): detail and contrast concentrated at the eyes/mouth, the rest left simple.
- Line weight hierarchy front-to-back (p51) supports a slightly heavier outline on the near side of the face in
  3/4 than on the far side.
