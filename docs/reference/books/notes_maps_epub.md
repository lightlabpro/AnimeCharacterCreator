# How to Draw Fantasy Art and RPG Maps - Jared Blando (IMPACT 2015, 270 epub pages) - reading notes (q20)

Read: full text layer (every page, 2 chunks) + every page image (8 combined sheets). A cartography book: no
figures or faces except a small statue icon (p132-134). Nothing on facial anatomy; recorded for completeness,
with the few general rendering rules that transfer.

## Core content
- Materials; light 2H framework, HB for forms, 2B for final dark boundaries (p17-21, p26-32).
- Coastlines, islands, lakes, bays; land/water balance (p23-32). Mountains as inverted V/W, largest in the middle
  of a chain, five to ten flank lines; hills as inverted U; forests as flat-bottomed triangles shaded on one
  consistent side; rivers start thin at tributaries and widen to the mouth, line pressure light to heavy
  (p34-61).
- Towns, cities, castles; typography (location labels straight, area labels follow contours, region labels
  faded to push them back) (p62-103). Landmarks, icons, heraldry (one symbol is plenty), boundaries (subtle,
  never covering icons), borders, corners, compass, legend (p104-222).
- Painting (p223-247): line hierarchy - thick for land boundaries and frames, medium for terrain, thin for
  detail (p227-229); base tone, then darks, then highlights, then lightest lights; "Contrast is what draws the
  eye, and this is increased the closer the darkest darks are to the lightest lights" (p235); pure black is
  never used unmixed - "pure blacks are unnatural" (p237); lightest lights sparingly (p239).
- Full map walkthrough in 28 steps (p248-267).

## What this means for head_mhs3_n56 (q20)
- No anatomy content. Transferable: a three-level line-weight hierarchy (outer silhouette thickest, feature
  lines medium, detail thinnest) and placing the darkest dark beside the lightest light only at the focal point
  (eyes). Avoid pure black fills in the mouth interior; use a dark mixed color so the interior keeps depth.
