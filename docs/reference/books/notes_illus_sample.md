# Illustrator's Guidebook by 21 Draw - SAMPLE (44 pp) - reading notes (q20)

Read: full text layer (every page) + every page image (5 3x3 sheets) + full-size p37 (profile head steps).
The sample covers front matter, Fundamentals (p9-13) and the first part of Figure Drawing: Tom Bancroft (male hero,
p16-25), Bleedman (cartoon female + head tutorial, p26-29), Warren Louw (ideal female, head side/front, flow,
contrapposto, study method, p30-43). It ends at p44 "END OF SAMPLE".

## Fundamentals (p10-13)
- Warm-up lines, dots, spirals; volume = know where the light comes from and render shadows consistently; tools.

## Bancroft (p16-25)
- Simplified anatomy; "light bulb" torso; head variations: start with the cranium circle and add U, V, wide or narrow
  jaws; vary nose length, eye size, mouth and ear placement; once you know the simple head shapes and how they relate
  you can draw the character from any angle (p20-21, illustrations A/B incl. side views).
- Straights are solid, angles are dynamic; tilt the head in poses (p22).
- Step 5 (p24): after the line drawing, ask what would make it stronger - he made the head smaller.

## Bleedman head tutorial (p29) - anime/cartoon female
- Start with a sphere, add the lower jaw, then the eye and nose guidelines; keep the eyes on their own layer so size
  and spacing can be adjusted - eye design carries personality, mood and style.

## Warren Louw head studies (p36-39)
- Side profile (p36-37): circle, divided in quarters; the face front lines go in the lower-left quarter; a half-size
  circle in the centre marks the flat temporal side; ear is an oval in the lower right of that circle, tilted, not
  vertical; ear top level with the start of the eyebrow, ear bottom level with the bottom of the nose; cheekbone line
  from mid-ear curving into the chin.
  - Bottom lip aligns with the forehead; chin lines up with the start of the eyebrow; the corner of the mouth is in
    line with the front of the eye; the nose projects most, the top lip second.
  - The angle of the nostril line is generally parallel to the angle of the mouth; philtrum slightly curved or
    vertical; upper lip rounded by fullness; the bottom lip turns into its underside; the chin is a round shape
    before it runs into the jaw line.
  - The eye in profile is drawn upper lash line first, then lower lashes, iris, upper lid; on p37 it is a small
    arrow/wedge open toward the front, set back behind the nose bridge.
  - Beginners make the distance eye-to-ear and ear-to-back of head too small.
- Front view (p38-39): the front head circle is smaller than the side one; the brow line is just below the circle
  centre, the nose line at the circle bottom, chin = (brow-to-nose) below the nose line; hairline = same distance
  above the brow; jaw curves in from the hairline-circle intersections to the nose line then to the chin.
  - Mouth "in line with the curves of the sides of the jaw".
  - V-line: brow ends, eye corners, nostril sides and top of the upper lip line up; keep the space between eyes and
    ears (do not compress it); the inner corner of the iris lines up with the mouth corner; nose sides line up with
    the start of the eyebrows; bottom of the eye level with the inner fold of the ear.
  - Common errors he lists: face too large for the head, eyes too big or wide, forehead too small, brows too high.

## Figure (p30-35, 40-43)
- Shapes first, then gesture/flow, weight, then skeleton and muscle; 7-head ideal female; neck 1/4 head; flow lines
  (S curve); contrapposto; study one pose by repetition and from memory.

## What this means for head_mhs3_n56 (q20)
- Mouth corner sits in line with the iris inner edge (front) and the front of the eye (profile): a wider mouth than
  our current open mouth; consistent with Edwards (corners under the pupils).
- Lower face: brow-to-nose = nose-to-chin. Check our nose-to-chin distance; if it is longer than brow-to-nose the
  lower face reads stretched.
- Profile eye: a small wedge set back from the bridge, not a frontal almond.
