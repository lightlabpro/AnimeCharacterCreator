# FORCE: Dynamic Life Drawing (10th anniversary ed.) - Michael D. Mattesi (CRC 2017, 234 pp) - reading notes (q20)

Read: full text layer (every page, 2 chunks) + every page image (26 3x3 sheets). Gesture and figure drawing;
the head appears mostly as a perspective box. PDF page numbers.

## Core content
- Key concepts: humanity, truth through opinion and exaggeration ("Copying leads to lying"), flow, passion, fear,
  risk, contrast creates interest (Disney), hierarchy pyramid - the big idea first, details last (p16-22).
- Ch1 Seeing life: Directional FORCE line (one curve = one force), Applied FORCE at the apex, the leading edge,
  rhythm = 2 directional + 1 applied, "Rabbit" and "Snake" errors (rhythm must cross the figure obliquely), C and S
  rib cage-to-pelvis templates, side view and leg templates ("outside thigh, inside knee, outside calf"), balance
  over the centre line, the head leads the turn and must agree with the back; the neck opposes the back through
  the sternocleidomastoid, never a straight tube (p24-100).
- Ch2 FORCEFUL form: perspective; "Fake perspective" box, one/two/three/four-point; the head drawn from a cube
  because its planes fix direction; on a two-point head the turning edge between front and side planes runs through
  the brow peak and temple, and nose underside and upper lip show three planes (p103-107). Surface lines (sculpt,
  not shade), turning edge, Blind FORCE drawing, overlap ("T" rule) vs tangents, heavier edges come forward,
  foreshortening (p117-160).
- Ch3 FORCEFUL shape: straight against curve, no mirrored bulges (sausage, tube, pinch are the don'ts), FORCE blob,
  silhouette first, leading edge in shape, hard bone / soft muscle alternation (head hard, neck soft...), no concave
  curves on the outer side of a limb, size for depth, reaction (p162-222).

## What this means for head_mhs3_n56 (q20)
- The face is a box before it is features: front and side planes meet at a turning edge through the brow peak.
  In 3/4 every feature lives on those planes; the far eye sits on the side-turning front plane, the near eye on
  the frontal plane (agrees with Cooney's SRS / Quasimodo warning).
- Shape design for the lower face: straight against curve, peaks never mirrored. A jaw line drawn as one long
  even curve reads as "stretched"; a straight cheek plane against a curved chin reads firmer.
- Upper lip under the nose shows its own plane (p106), supporting a separate shade plane for the upper lip.
