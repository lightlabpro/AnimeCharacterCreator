# How to Draw - Scott Robertson with Thomas Bertling (211 pp) - reading notes (q20)

Read: full text layer (every page, 4 chunks) + every page image (24 3x3 sheets). Industrial / entertainment design
perspective: objects, environments, aircraft, cars. No figure or face anatomy.

## Core content
- Ch1 materials and skills: ghost the line, draw with the whole arm, rotate the paper, draw lightly, draw through,
  practise straight lines, accelerating curves through waypoints, ellipses with their minor axis.
- Ch2 terminology: ground plane, station point, line of sight, picture plane; 60 deg cone of vision ~ 50 mm lens
  (feels natural); horizon = eye height; tilting the head gives 3-point.
- Ch3 techniques: divide/multiply rectangles with diagonals (auto-foreshortening), odd divisions, mirroring planes,
  tilted planes, 2D and 3D curves (2-curve combo).
- Ch4 grids: 1/2/3/5-point grid types, diagonal VP / station point method, same-size squares by rotating around an
  ellipse, transferring scale with reference points, Brewer method (VPs off the page), computer underlays (MODO,
  SketchUp), linear vs curvilinear (lens-distorted) grids.
- Ch5 ellipses: "The minor axis ... always points to the Vanishing Point that is perpendicular to the ellipse's
  surface ... like the axle of a wheel" (p71); the major axis does not pass through the centre of the perspective
  square; ellipse degree grows away from the horizon (0 deg on it, 90 deg straight below); a circle on any surface:
  first the minor axis perpendicular to the surface, then the degree (p72-73); hinging, subdividing, sloped surfaces.
- Ch6 volume: plan in draft views first; X-Y-Z section drawing (centerline Y section, top-view Z section, X sections,
  silhouette drawn tangent to all sections, p86); 2-curve combo; cutting volumes by projecting curves; radii (remove
  volume) and fillets (add volume); wrapping graphics; contour lines; line weight - "Where one line overlaps another,
  the weight of the overlapping foreground line is thicker" (p100); vignette (fade the far side); occlusion.
- Ch7 environments: linear perspective, occlusion and atmospheric perspective (lower contrast with distance), photo
  underlays, site planning, thumbnails with value, rule of thirds for the horizon, wide-angle grids.
- Ch8 aircraft and Ch9 wheeled vehicles: research, packaging / hard points, side view first, grids built from
  wheel ellipses (wheelbase ~3 wheels, width 2.5-3), stylised side views, tumblehome, belt/roof/shoulder lines,
  wheel wells as intersections, overlays for variations, wide-angle sketches.
- Ch10 mediums: ballpoint, marker, graphite, coloured pencil, Hi-Tec, brush pen, gouache, toned paper, digital.

## What this means for head_mhs3_n56 (q20)
- The iris is a circle on a sphere: at 3/4 and in profile its outline is an ellipse whose minor axis points along the
  gaze (the eye's own axis), not a horizontally squashed copy of the front shape. Our view keys compress the iris
  horizontally about its centre - right for the minor axis only while the eye looks straight ahead.
- Silhouette = line tangent to the sections: the 3/4 cheek line should be drawn from the face's own sections, so the
  far eye sits inside it rather than being cut by it.
- Overlap line weight (thicker foreground line) is a cue the toon outline could use (upper lip over the opening).
