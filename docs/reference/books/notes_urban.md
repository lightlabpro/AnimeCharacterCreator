# The Urban Sketching Handbook: Understanding Perspective - Stephanie Bower (115 pp) - reading notes (q20)

Page numbers below are the printed ones (PDF page = printed + 2; rules_urban.json uses PDF pages).
Read: full text layer (every page, 2 chunks) + every page image (13 3x3 sheets). Location sketches of architecture
by ~40 sketchers with thumbnail perspective diagrams; no figures beyond tiny people.

## Core content
- Key I Basics: tools; terms (parallel, perpendicular, elevation = flat frontal view); proportions measured with
  the pencil as height:width ratios; start from the Shape of the Face (one rectangle), look for squares.
- Key II Spatial principles: diminishing (BOTH size and spacing shrink with distance - p29 diagram: equal spacing
  with shrinking heights shows little depth); converging (parallels meet at a VP on the eye-level line);
  horizon line = eye level; foreshortening - "the closer something is to your eye level or vanishing point, the
  flatter it will appear" (p34-37); stacked balcony circles read as ellipses flatter near eye level (p36).
- Key III Types: eye level / bird's-eye / worm's-eye; one-point, two-point (VPs often off the page - measure the
  angle), three-point (verticals converge when looking up/down), rotated planes give VPs along the eye line,
  tilted planes (stairs, roofs) give VPs straight above/below the main VP. Common error: VP too high.
- Key IV Build in layers: thumbnail, block the whole size first in light lines, then smaller shapes, then detail,
  then colour; Shape of the Face / of the Space / of the Box recipes.
- Key V More: ellipse from a square in perspective - "the widest point of the ellipse ... is offset from the
  square's centerline" (p82); "Ellipses don't have points!" (p88); arches = spring line + circle part;
  domes = stacked ellipses on one centre line; reflections fold at the water level.
- Galleries: wide-angle/fish-eye curving, hatching textures, watercolour light; challenges list.

## What this means for head_mhs3_n56 (q20)
- The iris turned at 3/4 or profile is a foreshortened circle: an ellipse whose widest part sits toward the near
  side of its box, rounded at the ends - never a pointed sliver. Our profile iris and eye white become thin
  pointed slivers (eyside q20v5) - this is exactly what "ellipses don't have points" warns against.
- Foreshortening flattens the side of the face turned away; the far eye at 3/4 should be both smaller and closer to
  its neighbours (diminishing applies to size and spacing together).
- Block the whole face proportion first (nose-mouth-chin spacing) before tuning parts.
