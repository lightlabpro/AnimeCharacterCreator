# How to Draw Manga: Giant Robots - Hikaru Hayashi / Go Office (129 pp) - reading notes (q20)

Read: full text layer (every page, OCR - the scan has little text) + every page image (15 3x3 sheets), plus a
close look at p119 (human / robot faces front, profile, back).

## Core content
- Section 1 basic robot design: start from a human figure; beveled-edge face (a beveled shape simplifies the
  forehead / cheekbone / jaw planes); replace or omit face parts; eye types (crescent, polygonal, round, goggle,
  lens - "use of multiple levels of tone and addition of light will create the impression of a three-dimensional
  lens"); mouth designs; design from the human skull; "If the robot's mouth is open, the bottom should be shaded to
  create a sense of depth" (p18); nose and ear shapes in front/side/up/down views; ears drawn near the middle of the
  head read human; necks, trunks (cylinder, box, board), chests, hips, joints (crane arms), hands, legs (higher
  knees look better), metal rendering (high contrast = glossy, low = matte, dual contour lines for a shiny edge).
- Section 2 combat robots: armour-based design, weapons, helmets, transport units, colour as grey values, flames,
  explosions, combining machines.
- Section 3 female robots: body lines (straight vs curved), mannequin type, faces and hair (p119: the human manga
  profile - the eye is a short wedge open toward the front, set well back from the profile line, about as wide as
  tall; iris a narrow upright oval at the front of the eye), fashion-based designs.

## What this means for head_mhs3_n56 (q20)
- An open mouth needs its lower interior shaded to read deep (same rule as Kistler's undershadow).
- Profile eye (p119): wedge, set back, width about equal to height, iris a narrow upright oval at the front edge -
  our base profile eye is a full front-view almond about twice as wide as tall.
- A lens/eye reads 3D through stacked tones plus a highlight.
