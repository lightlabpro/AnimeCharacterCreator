# Pen & Ink Drawing (Pen & Ink Themes) - Frank J. Lohan (Dover 2013 / 1981, 138 epub pages) - reading notes (q20)

Read: full text layer (every page, 2 chunks) + every page image (4 combined sheets). A landscape / still-life
pen book; no face construction. Faces appear only as small examples (horn player p71, lineman p72, colt p70,
horse heads p83-85, Japanese girl vignette p93, gargoyle and wood sprite p119-120).

## Core content
- Tools and strokes: crowquill, fountain and technical pens, brush, scratchboard; hatching, crosshatching,
  stipple (p10-12). Build darks light to dark in layers (goose p77, junco p78).
- Minimise outlines in the final ink (p21); the lit edge can be implied with no line at all, only the shadow side
  is drawn (birches p88-89: "It is only your imagination that sees an edge on the left-hand sides of the trees
  where the sunlight strikes them"). Glare is shown by omitting the hard edge (p49).
- Less is more: suggest detail and let the viewer complete it (p17, p21, p49, p69, p116). "Getting out of" a
  drawing - detail fades away from the centre of interest (p44, p49).
- Depth from overlap: leave a thin white gap where a nearer element passes in front of a farther one; touching
  lines destroy depth (p88 rigging, p100 fence wire, p111 field grass).
- Focus by contrast: the greatest contrast and finest lines go where attention should land (horse face p83);
  alternate light and dark bands to separate receding masses (p97-98, p127).
- Fog and haze flatten distant forms to monotone silhouettes (p61-63, p131).
- Hatch lines follow the surface: curved for concave/convex, straight for flat (skull p108; saguaro p104-105).
- Symmetric subjects - "buildings and faces" - need precise construction; asymmetric natural subjects tolerate
  variation (p92, p132). Check proportion/perspective in a mirror before inking (p18).
- A two-tone face under a spotlight reads best with almost no interior detail (horn player p71).
- Good design = a symmetrical space subdivided asymmetrically with taste (p94).

## What this means for head_mhs3_n56 (q20)
- Faces are named with buildings as subjects that tolerate no sloppy symmetry (p92) - matches our L/R mirror
  check of eyes in front view.
- Toon outline practice: a lit contour can drop its line entirely while the shadow side carries the edge
  (p88-89). Supports a lighter or absent outline on the lit cheek and heavier line on the shadow side, and not
  outlining the upper lip where a shadow shape already defines it.
- Overlap gap (p111): where the nose or far cheek passes in front of the far eye in 3/4, a clean separation
  line/gap sells depth; merged lines flatten it.
- Few interior marks on a brightly lit young face (p71) - same message as Hart and Pavelec.
