# DragonArt - J "NeonDragon" Peffer (IMPACT 2005, 130 pp) - reading notes (q20)

Read: full text layer (every page, 2 chunks) + every page image (15 3x3 sheets).

## Core content
- Basics: everything from simple shapes; shading by light source (planes give hard edges, rounds give gradual
  shadow); overlap + size + atmosphere for depth (p12-15).
- Part 1 dragons: basic dragon from a line of motion; shape language - circles = friendly/soft, triangles =
  harsh/evil, boxes = massive (p22-23). Faces and expressions: round pupil = warm/intelligent/human, slit = beast,
  none = alien; the eye ridge acts as an eyebrow; a rigid jaw shows emotion only through a fleshy lip that curls;
  happy needs wide eyes (a grin with angry brows reads evil); laughing pushes the cheek up into the eye
  (p24-25). Heads in side / front / 3/4: "The head does not morph just because it's seen at a different angle";
  front view must be symmetrical; set the eyes behind the brow ridges so they sit in sockets, never pasted on the
  surface (p26-31). Cel vs soft shading (p28). Limbs, paws, talons (set nails back into the digits), bodies from
  cat/bird/lizard anatomy, skeleton, wings as arms, folded wings keep the same anatomy, Western and Eastern
  dragons (p32-73). Line weight: thick lines for major shapes / foreground, thin for details (p27, p35).
- Part 2 details: horns, tusks, frills/fins, scales (pebble, curved-line, varied), tails, feathers, fur ("sweet
  spots": simplified oval highlights and blocks of shadow move the eye better than photo-accuracy, p77) (p74-85).
- Part 3 creatures: gargoyle, Pegasus, Chimera, kirin, phoenix, griffin, basilisk, wyvern, sea serpent, creature
  design questions, quick guide (p86-127).

## What this means for head_mhs3_n56 (q20)
- "The head does not morph with the angle" (p28) - our 3/4 eye problem is exactly a morph: the eye shape changes
  shape across views instead of turning with the head. Eyes must sit in sockets behind the brow ridge (p29), which
  supports showing the far eye partly behind the bridge/brow rather than stretched on the surface.
- A smiling mouth with wide eyes reads happy; expression lives in the lip line, not the jaw (p24-25).
- Cel shading uses sharp transitions with "sweet spot" shapes (p28, p77): one deliberate shadow shape for the
  upper lip and mouth interior is right for our toon look.
