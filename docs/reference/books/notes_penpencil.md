# Pen and Pencil Drawing Techniques - Harry Borgman (Dover 2002 / Watson-Guptill 1989, 220 PDF pages) - reading notes (q20)

Read: full text layer (every page, 3 chunks; the OCR of this scan is poor, so captions were checked against the
images) + every page image (7 combined sheets) + full-size close-ups of the portrait pages (p36, p37, p114,
p120). Page numbers are PDF pages.

## Core content
- Part 1 pencil: tools, sharpening, papers (smooth, rough, charcoal) and how each changes the same portrait
  (p10-21); stroke techniques - thin, broad, varied, parallel (p22-37); building tone (p38-45); simplifying a
  scene to two to four values plus paper (p46-49); graphite, charcoal/pastel, wax pencil technique catalogues
  (p50-83); scribbling, hatching, dissolved tone, subtractive (eraser) technique (p84-91); color pencil with
  washes, dyes, gouache, oil crayon, acrylic (p92-123); technical tips (p124-127).
- Part 2 ink: pens and nibs chart, papers (p128-135); stroke exercises, ruling, drawing objects with few lines
  (p137-143); broad pen, brush, markers (p144-150); tonal techniques, interpreting gray values into line,
  sketch-to-tone, outline plus hatching, crosshatching (p151-170); brush line, scratchboard, toned paper,
  colored inks and markers (p171-205); correcting ink, reference photos, graphite transfer sheet (p206-217).
- Portrait demonstration (p34-37): a diagrammatic sketch first decides proportions and sizes - "the most
  important stage"; dark accents go only on eyes, nose, mouth, under the ear and chin - "if incorrectly placed,
  they can ruin your drawing" (p36).
- Value simplification: break any subject into a few flat values before rendering; the white paper counts as a
  value (p46-49, p152-153).

## Observed in the portrait images (my reading of the pictures, not quoted text)
- p114 (profile, "Sandra"): the eye is a short wedge set well back from the nose bridge; the upper lip sits in
  shadow under the nose while the lower lip catches light; the jaw is a single smooth line to the ear.
- p120 (3/4, "Sukri"): the far eye is much shorter than the near eye and partly cut by the bridge of the nose;
  the upper lip is the dark plane, the teeth show as a light shape just under it, and the shadow of the upper
  lip falls across the top of the teeth - the mouth reads as recessed, with depth.
- p36-37 (3/4, young man): eye sockets are shadow masses under the brow; few lines on the cheek.
