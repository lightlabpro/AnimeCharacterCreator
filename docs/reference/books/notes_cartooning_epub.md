# Modern Cartooning - Christopher Hart (2013, 388 pp) - reading notes (q20)

Read: full text layer (every page, 2 chunks) + every page image (44 3x3 sheets). Western TV-animation style
(Fairly OddParents / Total Drama look), not manga, but the head-construction and feature rules carry over.
Page numbers below are PDF pages.

## Core content
- Ch1 head shapes: the head construction stays visible as a feature in the final design (p11). Circle = youth
  ("Circles tend to make everyone look young", p12). Centre line + low eye line, features gathered at the
  crossing (p14-15); "The lower you go, the cuter he gets" (p19). Young teen: big round eyes, small nose,
  draw the top lip first to set the expression, then the bottom lip (p21-23); nose, mouth and ears kept small to
  favour the eyes (p26). Mid-teen: head smaller relative to the features, less surface around them (p27); almond
  eyes tilted up, thick upper lids, brows arched high (p28-30). Circle at angles: shape stays, only guidelines
  move (p33). Adults: raise the eye line, so everything below reads as cheek and jowl (p34-35). Older = shorter
  neck (p41).
- Ch2 features: eye shapes circle / tall oval (pupil stays a circle) / flattened oval / severe / almond
  (p46-47); "Her top eyelid is always darker than the bottom" (p47). Four eye rules (p51): same tilt, same shape,
  same level, equal distance from nose and face edge. Pupils: size, placement and shine change the read;
  enlarged pupils = joy (p52-53). Attractive female eyes: upper lid covers the top third of the eyeball and is
  thick (p56). Closed eyes up = happy, down = confident (p57). Nose problem: a pointed nose cheats into side view
  even on a front head (p60-61). Mouths drawn in 3/4 first, then profile (p62-69): open-mouth anger has uneven
  lips; more teeth = more intense laugh (p63); "About to cry": the line above the upper lip lengthens (p64);
  bottom-teeth-only drags the mouth down at the end (p64); jovial smile minimizes the lower lip (p65). Female lips
  (p70): petite expressions over broad ones; classic version both lips curve up, bottom lip thickest. Female smile
  (p71-73): top lip horizontal or curving slightly down, wide and deep, lips thin when stretched but keep thickness
  in the middle, smile creases curl up at the ends. Eyes and mouth together on the guidelines (p75-77).
- Ch3 beyond the circle: narrowing the jaw makes a face more feminine (p86); egg head - large forehead, slender
  jaw (p87); thin faces for teens and young adults, jaw grows to half the face length vs children (p94-95); ears
  below the eye line read younger / funnier (p97); classic cartoon head with a single pushed-out cheek "jag-step";
  cheek low for kids, a bit below halfway for teens, middle for adults (p100-106); cute girl has abrupt angle shifts
  and a tapered chin, ear rarely rises above the cheek (p107-109); neck set back from the chin, not straight
  under it (p111). Sculpting: add mass / slice it away (p126-128); too many planes hurt - four planes beat eight
  (p129-130); convex vs concave forehead (p131); sculpting variants (p132-135); sketching loosely (p136-139);
  visual effects (p140-142).
- Ch4 medium shots: head and body must share one look; kawaii hat girl - eyes take most of the face, small mouth,
  eyes lifted at the sides (p145-147). Bold lines: "Draw straight lines very straight and curved lines very
  curved" (p154). First snowflake: "When the head tilts up and the eyes look upward, they change from circular to
  oval because of perspective" (p165, p170); straighten the lower lid (p166); gently flowing hairline (p168).
- Ch5 universal body: bean body (old), rubbery rectangle torso and its variants - hourglass, athletic top-heavy,
  bruiser, skinny uneven stretch, V-type, big body (one side straight one curved), compact torso; single-shape
  figures; clothing as the body shape (overcoat, pajama top, bell cape); female body = chest oval + trunk + hip
  bowl, thin overall so the head reads big; side view thinnest; 3/4 view near breast front, far breast side
  (p172-247).
- Ch6-7 finished characters and body types (shopper, goofy gal, gift box overlap, dorky dad, moms, senior,
  weightlifter); "De-provement Zone" - stop before over-refining (p249); enhancing face: "Make her upper lip go
  straight across. Create her smile with the curve of the bottom lip" (p279); thinner figure = longer arms (p292).
- Ch8 line of action: curve, reverse (S) curve, half line of action, hard angles; stretching/condensing the body
  (p302-343). Ch9 fine points: features gathered in the middle and below halfway make a head chubby/young (p348);
  heads-tall measure (p350); scenes, bookending, framing devices, ruler vs freehand never mixed (p354-384).

## What this means for head_mhs3_n56 (q20)
- Lower face stretch: Hart ties a long jaw to age (jaw = half the face only from teens on, p95) and raised eyes to
  adult jowls (p34). Our teen/young-adult hero should keep the features gathered and the jaw no longer than about
  half the face; this agrees with chardesigner p120 (stretching the jaw down ages the face).
- Upper lip: for a pretty/young face the upper lip is straight or dips slightly, and the smile curve lives in the
  lower lip (p71-73, p279). Keep the upper lip a flat, clean shape with its own dark band (our UpperLipShade), not a
  curved bow.
- Open mouth: teeth count scales intensity (p63); the lower lip is minimised in a jovial smile (p65).
- Eyes at angles: an eye only turns oval where perspective demands it (head tilt / 3/4), the pupil stays round on
  a tall-oval eye (p46, p170) - supports keeping the iris from stretching in our 3/4 view.
- Eye rules p51 (same tilt, shape, level, equal spacing) are a direct check list for the front view.
