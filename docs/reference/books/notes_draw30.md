# You Can Draw in 30 Days - Mark Kistler (257 pp) - reading notes (q20)

Read: full text layer (every page) + every page image (29 3x3 sheets). Pages 249-257 are blank practice pages.

## Core content
- Nine Fundamental Laws of depth (p11-12): foreshortening, placement, size, overlapping, shading (opposite the light),
  shadow (cast on the ground), contour lines (wrap round forms), horizon line, density (far = lighter, less detail).
  "It is impossible to draw a three-dimensional image without applying one or more of these laws."
- Lessons 1-21: sphere with blended shading and a "hot spot"; nook-and-cranny shadows (dark, small, under and behind
  near objects - "separate, define and identify"); undershadows push a recessed part deeper (p57, p69); overlapping
  trumps size, placement can trump both (p29-30, p106, p156); thickness rule for openings - "if the window is on the
  right, the thickness is on the right" (p37, p76); peeking lines (the short back edge visible inside an opening)
  are what make a shape read 3-D (p56, p91, p145, p150); contour lines curve more as they recede and set the
  direction of a tube (p130-136); flat planes get one even tone, curved surfaces get blended tone (p154-155).
- Lessons 22-27: one- and two-point perspective.
- Lesson 28 human face (p217-226): traced da Vinci: the near eye larger at 3/4, upper lid overlapping the iris, upper
  lip "dips down" in the centre under the philtrum ridges, lower lip = two round shaded spheres, nose tip kept light,
  nostril shaped by blended tone not a hard line, mouth centre line = two S curves. Front grid: eyes at the middle,
  nose halfway eyes-chin, lips halfway nose-chin, five eye-widths across, mouth corners under the pupil centres,
  neck from the nose line, hairline halfway eyes-top.
- Lesson 29 eye (p227-232): lemon shape; iris a perfect circle tucked under the upper lid (overlap); visible thickness
  of the lower lid rim; pupil highlight; five shading areas - under the upper lid across the eyeball, above the lower
  lid on the eyeball, the lid crease, the socket under the eye, and the nook in the inner corner.
- Lesson 30 hand (p233-238): applies all nine laws.

## What this means for head_mhs3_n56 (q20)
- The open mouth needs "peeking" thickness: the visible inner edge of the upper lip/teeth and the back wall, plus a
  nook-and-cranny dark at the corners and under the upper teeth. Flat bands of teeth and tongue with no undershadow
  are exactly what reads 2-D. Teeth: shade one band under the upper lip (undershadow); tongue: blended, darker at the
  back. Thickness rule: on the 3/4 view the inner wall shows on the far side of the opening.
- Upper lip: a dip under the philtrum and a plane that faces down -> it needs a nook shadow line under it.
- Eye at 3/4: iris stays a circle tucked under the lid (foreshortened to an ellipse only by the turn), near eye larger.
