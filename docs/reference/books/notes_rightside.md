# The New Drawing on the Right Side of the Brain - Betty Edwards (315 pp) - reading notes (q20)

Read: full text layer (every page, c00-c06) + every page image (35 3x3 sheets) + full-size p188, p212, p217.
Page numbers = PDF pages (book folio = PDF page - 16 in chapters 9-10).

## What the book is (p1-160)
- Preface/intro: five perceptual skills - edges, spaces, relationships (sighting: angles/proportion), lights and
  shadows, gestalt; plus memory and the imagination "dialogue" (p17-27, p275-277).
- Upside-down copying (Picasso's Stravinsky), Vase/Faces, pure and modified contour drawing, picture plane,
  negative space, Basic Unit, sighting angles relative to vertical/horizontal (p50-159). Mostly method, not
  anatomy; useful for us as a validation method: compare to the ref by ratios to one Basic Unit, not by memory.
- Childhood symbol system (p68-85): people draw a stored symbol of an eye/nose/mouth instead of the seen shape;
  the symbolic eye is drawn frontal in every view (the "Egyptian" error in our q20 complaint is exactly this).

## Chapter 9 - Portrait proportions, profile (p177-207)
- Chopped-off-skull error (p183-190): beginners make the skull too small and the features too big. The eye
  level is at HALF of the whole head (eye-to-chin = eye-to-top of skull); with thick hair the top is bigger.
  The features occupy a surprisingly SMALL part of the head (p196, p265 "do not enlarge these features").
- Profile blank is an odd egg, different from the front blank (p189-190).
- Ear in profile (p191-192): eye level-to-chin = back of the eye to the back of the ear (isosceles right
  triangle). Bottom of the ear is level with the space between nose and mouth. The back of the neck joins the
  skull at about the level of the nose/upper lip - higher than people think; the neck is wider than drawn.
- Profile mouth (p200): the centreline of the mouth is the only true edge; the upper/lower lip contours are
  only colour changes - draw them lightly. In profile the centreline of the mouth DESCENDS relative to
  horizontal (toward the back).
- Shape under the nostril is a specific negative shape (p201). Ears are bigger than you expect (p201).
- Profile eye (p204, Fig 9-38/39): lids have thickness, the eyeball sits behind the lids; the whole eye shape
  SLANTS BACK at an angle from the front of the profile because of how the eyeball sits in the bony socket.
  Upper lashes grow down then curve up. Draw the white as a negative shape instead of the iris symbol.
- Neck: negative space in front of the neck; the back of the neck joins the skull about at nose/mouth level.

## Chapter 10 - Light logic, frontal and three-quarter proportions (p208-242)
- Light logic (p209-210): highlight, cast shadow (darkest, e.g. under nose, LIPS, chin), reflected light,
  crest shadow. Fuseli: cast shadows by the nose and the lips. Courbet: shadow under the nose and under the
  LOWER lip; "almost no information in the shadowed areas" - shadow shapes, not detail, make the form (p216-217).
- Frontal view (p225-227, Fig 10-24): eye level at half; head width at eye level = 5 eye widths; space between
  the eyes = one eye; nose bottom = point of the inverted triangle from the outer eye corners; MOUTH CENTRELINE
  about 1/3 of the way from nose to chin; nostril edges under the inner eye corners ("noses are wider than you
  think"); MOUTH CORNERS under the PUPILS ("mouths are wider than you think"); ear top at eye level, ear bottom
  at the space between nose and mouth; neck almost as wide as the jaw.
- Three-quarter view (p228-233):
  - Nose seen from top and side looks wide; the two sides of the face are different widths; the turned-side eye
    is narrower and a different SHAPE; the mouth from centre to corner is SHORTER on the turned side and shaped
    differently (p229).
  - Central axis passes through the centre of the bridge of the nose and the middle of the upper lip, like a wire
    through the nose; eye level stays at right angles to it (p230).
  - Pose test: the nose tip nearly coincides with the contour of the turned cheek (p230).
  - Near eye: inside corner on the eye line, set one full eye-width away from the edge of the nose - the most
    common error is putting it too close to the nose (p232).
  - Nostril edge under the inner eye corner along the axis; mouth corner relative to the eye; the curve of the
    mouth centreline carries the expression; lip outlines are light, not true edges; on the turned side the
    centreline has its own curve (p232).
  - Ear in 3/4: eye level-to-chin = FRONT of the eye to the back of the ear (p233).
  - Beginners widen the narrow side and narrow the near side until the 3/4 face turns into a frontal face (p238).
- Summary list p237: mouth corners fall under the pupils; note the outer corners of the mouth where much of the
  expression lives; ear bottoms at about the upper lip.

## Chapter 11-12, afterword, postscript, glossary, bibliography, index (p243-315)
- Colour: hue/value/intensity; complements; a coloured ground as the middle value; heighten lights only where
  light falls (p243-271). Values matter more than colours ("if the values are right the colour cannot help but
  be right"). Read fully; no anatomy.
- Zen chapter, handwriting afterword, postscript, glossary (Blank, Central Axis, Eye level), bibliography, index,

## What this means for head_mhs3_n56 (q20)
1. Mouth too small: the book's frontal rule puts the mouth corners under the pupils. Anime shrinks the mouth at
   rest, but the open/talking mouth must still read wide enough; check corner x against the pupil x (front) and
   keep the 3/4 near half longer than the far half (p229) - matches Crilley and animeoutline.
2. Lower face stretched: realistic mouth centreline at 1/3 nose-to-chin. Anime puts the mouth closer to the
   middle (Crilley: midway). Measure our chin length as a ratio of the eye-level unit; the eye line must stay at
   or above half of the head (never chopped skull = never giant lower face).
3. "Egyptian" eyes in side/3/4: the eye must slant back from the profile line and narrow on the turned side; a
   frontal almond on a side head is the classic symbol error.
4. Upper lip "missing": the lip line is a colour change; what gives the upper lip form is the plane change and
   the cast shadow under the nose/upper lip and under the lower lip. Our toon shading needs a shadow shape on the
   upper-lip plane (it faces down) rather than an outline.
