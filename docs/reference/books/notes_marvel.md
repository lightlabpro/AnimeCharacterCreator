# How to Draw Comics the Marvel Way - Stan Lee & John Buscema (138 pp) - reading notes (q20)

Read: full text layer (every page) + every page image (16 3x3 sheets).

## Ch 1-3 (p8-35): tools, terms, form, perspective
- FLATNESS ruins a drawing: everything must have thickness/depth; "think through the object" (p16).
- Every object reduces to sphere, cube, cylinder; faces too: "this one is also based upon a cylinder - which has been
  cut off at the sides" (p19); shading (black tones) on the solids gives roundness and mass (p20).
- Horizon line = eye level; 1/2/3-point perspective; circle in a tilted square becomes an oval (p25-32).

## Ch 4-7 (p36-72): figure, action, foreshortening
- Heroic 8.75 heads; female head slightly smaller; stick figure -> cylinders -> flesh out; centre line first;
  exaggerate action. Foreshortening chapter intro p66: "Without knowledge of foreshortening, all your figures could
  end up looking like they were drawn on pyramids by the ancient Egyptians!" - i.e. every part must turn in depth
  (the exact problem Sammy describes with our side/3/4 eyes).

## Ch 8 - Drawing the human head (p73-94)
- Profile head fits a square, with nose and part of the chin protruding; eyes midway between top of the dome and
  the bottom of the chin; divide into quarters: nose in the second quarter up from the chin, ears at about the same
  level; in front view the jaw slopes so the bottom of the skull is much narrower than the top (p74).
- Skull boxes at many angles (p75): the planes (front, side, underside of jaw) are drawn as a turned box.
- Hero head (p76): five eyes wide; one eye between the eyes; MOUTH WIDTH from an equilateral triangle from the nose
  bridge through the nostril sides to the mouth line; chin width from a triangle from under the nose through the
  lower lip corners; keep the nose small, chin strong; "notice the curve of the upper lip - and just a small simple
  line for the lower lip".
- Female profile (p82-83): head in a square, eye line halfway; nose short, tilts out and up; cheek curve from the ear
  to the front, halfway between nose bottom and chin; "Place the mouth well forward from the skull"; lower lip fuller
  than upper lip, "while the upper lip juts out farther forward"; nostril on a line from mouth to eye line; forehead
  always rounded; lashes a solid mass.
- Female front (p84-85): egg; eye line midway; five eyes wide; equilateral triangle from outer eye corners to the
  centre line places the mouth; nose about 1/3 of the way up from the top of the lip to the eye line; nose a little
  narrower than one eye and tilted up; mouth width from lines from the nose top past the nostrils; upper and lower lip
  positioned by continuing the cheek line through the mouth area; outer eye corners slightly higher than inner.
- Expressions (p86-87): change only mouth, eyes and brows; no extra lines.
- Do/don't (p90-91): eyelashes as a mass; eye tilted up at the outside; DON'T draw the eye too long and narrow; don't
  let eyes droop; nose one smooth line, no bumps, small nostrils; mouth with a pleasing shape; ALWAYS place the upper
  lip farther forward than the lower one; don't make the upper lip too thin or too far forward; don't make the chin
  too weak or too prominent; don't draw bow lips or angular lips; don't let the lower lip jut forward.
- Female heads in many positions (p92): the nose always tilts up; the mouth and eyes follow the turned box.

## Ch 9-12 (p95-138): composition, page, cover, inking
- Simple unified design shapes; camera angles; never centre everything; varied figure sizes.
- Inking: lines heavier on the underside of bodies away from the light give solidity (p126); one definite light
  source; black masses on one side give roundness (p129); keep the black pattern simple.

## What this means for head_mhs3_n56 (q20)
- The "Egyptian" eye complaint is the foreshortening failure Buscema names: our eye shapes must turn with the head
  box (narrow and slant on the turned side, wedge in profile), not stay frontal.
- Upper lip must project forward of the lower lip in profile and carry its own plane; a missing upper lip reads as
  no upper lip form + no shadow under it. Underside planes (under nose, under lower lip, under jaw) get the shadow.
- Mouth width check: triangle from the nose bridge through the nostril sides.
- Face = cylinder cut at the sides: features wrap around a curved front, so the 3/4 mouth curves with the face.
