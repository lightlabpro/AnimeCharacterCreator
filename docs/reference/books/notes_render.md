# How to Render - Scott Robertson with Thomas Bertling (274 pp) - reading notes (q20)

Read: full text layer (every page, 4 chunks) + every page image (31 3x3 sheets). Light, shadow and reflectivity for
design rendering; one organic creature demo and one eye demo by Neville Page.

## Core content
- Ch1: objects have no outlines - silhouettes and surfaces read through value and colour changes.
- Ch2 light types: "value change = form change" (p19); hard vs soft light (shadow edge); falloff; occlusion
  ("wherever two adjoining surfaces come together to block the light", p24); rim light / half-light; multiple lights;
  reflected (bounce) light; sunbeams; atmospheric perspective. Shadow construction: light plane = light direction +
  shadow direction, sticks, shadow origin, sunlight (parallel) vs local light (radiating), positive/negative sunlight,
  shadows over obstacles, overhangs, openings, complex volumes, multiple lights.
- Ch3 geo forms: the 1-2-3 read (top lightest, side mid, shadow side darkest; light high and to one side, p53-54);
  "halfway to black" for shadow values (p55); value by light-ray angle of incidence; reflected light lightens
  shadow sides and is coloured by the surface it bounces from; double bounce; curved surfaces - light side,
  terminator (where rays are tangent), core shadow ("the dark band ... that starts at the terminator and gets lighter
  onto the shadow side", p66), passive vs reflective highlight, reflected light, occlusion shadow ("darkest part of
  the cast shadow ... cavities and crevasses", p68); cylinders, cones, spheres (wrap the shading around the minor
  axis pointing to the light, p82); shadow edge softness grows with distance from the caster (p87); backgrounds
  change apparent value.
- Ch4 complex volumes: simple curved surfaces as cylinder pieces, accelerating gradations, equal values for equal
  orientation, X-Y-Z sections, workflow (core and cast shadow first, then primary shapes, then details), cut lines
  (highlight on the edge facing the light), texture shows most on the Number 2 side, detail gradients.
- Ch5 objects: vehicle lighting, mech, character (rabbit: primary light + weaker rim light; lighting studies first),
  value vs colour (a saturated colour needs a darker white point), Neville Page organic creature - "The upper lip
  is one very long and thin wrapping cylinder", the brow over the eye "is cylindrical ... mindful of the core shadow
  and the consequential cast shadow" (p141), skin folds = more cylinders; environments with John Park and Robh Ruppel
  (few values first, atmospheric perspective).
- Ch6 photo reference library. Ch7-9 reflections: angle of incidence equal in / equal out, plotting by mirroring,
  concave stretches / convex compresses, perceived reflectivity vs value (dark reads shinier), Fresnel effect,
  reflection flipping, pools and puddles, reflections of the light, double bounce, cast shadows on shiny surfaces,
  indoor and outdoor layering. Neville Page eyes (p205-208): dark eyeball, the reflective layer maps the environment
  onto the eye; "the basic trick of a singular reflective highlight".
- Ch10 materials (metallic paint, translucence / subsurface scattering - lower contrast on skin-like materials,
  glass, luminescence, water, chrome, metals, wood, rust, leather, cloth, carbon fibre, weathering, camera effects,
  depth of field). Ch11 examples (2D over 3D).

## What this means for head_mhs3_n56 (q20)
- Upper lip: a thin cylinder lying across the mouth - it needs its own core shadow on the down-facing side and a
  small cast shadow on what lies under it (the teeth / the opening). Our new upper-lip shade band is the core-shadow
  part; the undershadow band under the teeth stands in for the cast shadow / occlusion.
- Open mouth interior = a crevice: occlusion shadows darkest where surfaces meet (the corners, under the teeth).
- Skin is translucent: keep the toon shadow contrast moderate (subsurface scattering lowers core/cast contrast).
- Eyes: the highlight is a reflection of the light/environment - its position follows the view, not the light alone.
