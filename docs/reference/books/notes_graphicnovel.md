# Writing and Illustrating the Graphic Novel - Daniel Cooney (Barron's 2011, 160 pp) - reading notes (q20)

Read: full text layer (every page, 4 chunks, mostly OCR) + every page image (18 3x3 sheets; the drawing pages
p67-72 at full size, because the OCR missed the "Drawing eyes" sidebar). Page numbers are PDF pages.

## Core content
- Ch1 character: observe and sketch real people; protagonist / supporting / incidental; hero and villain traits;
  character profile questions; visual cues and body language; the character brief (good vs bad); developing a
  look from roughs to a consistent final (p15-24).
- Ch2 storytelling: outline and research, premise, three-act structure, conflict types, suspense vs surprise
  (Hitchcock), MacGuffin, Marvel method vs full script, writing for an artist, pacing (one major action per panel),
  McCloud's panel-to-panel transitions, ways to slow down / speed up, dialogue and word counts (p26-58).
- Ch3 drawing (John Heebink, p61-72): 10 figure-drawing principles, balance and centre line, weight distribution,
  foreshortening (glass-ellipse demo), body language catalogue (p65-66).
  - "Simplified rotatable solids" (SRS): hold a simple 3D head solid in mind so every view is consistent. The
    "accidental Cubist" failure: shapes from one view misapplied to another (p67). Heads are a little taller than
    deep, front and side planes slightly flattened, ears behind the vertical side centre line (p68).
  - "Quasimodo effect": each feature drawn whole, as if from a different direction, gives a monster (p68).
  - Eyes are pushed back relative to the brows and the nose bridge; place the nose after the eyes (p68).
  - Drawing eyes sidebar (p68, image only): treat eyes as spheres; the lids are "semicircular balconies hugging
    the sphere". Lid lines look straight only where they pass between the viewer and the core of the eyeball; as
    they move toward the eyeball's horizon they curve more and more, because of foreshortening. Draw the opening
    as a leaf shape from any angle. Eye lines curve hard near the edge of the eyeball, "watch for this in profile
    and near-profile views". Up-shot: eyes appear to sag at their outer ends.
  - Three-quarter view (p69): the far eye is a little foreshortened, a little shorter and closer to a side view.
    Method: place the far eye shorter than you think; a "third eye" space-keeper beside it, a little longer;
    the near eye longest and biggest. The far corner of the far eye is blunted, the near corner of the near eye
    extended to near full length and pointiness. The nose bridge is a wedge glued on the third eye and decides
    whether it covers part of the far eye. Never cram the far eye into the visible area past the bridge; that puts
    it "too forward or far out". Brows sit ahead of the eyes and their centre line shifts outward. Simple anime
    styles exaggerate this unequal eye length.
  - Facial contours (p70): the jaw/cheek contour is usually subtle and smooth, especially on women in frontal
    views. Common profile mistakes: dropped ears, sloping forehead, heavy brow, eye and brow set too deep, the
    pupil not centred in the iris, a "mullet nose" with the nostril pushed back onto the face.
  - Hair as shape first, follow growth direction (p70); hands (p71-72).
  - Perspective (p73-82): one, two and three point, cone of vision, right-angle station point, an ellipse's widest
    part sits slightly in front of the square's centre (p78). Photo reference without copying (p83-86).
- Ch4 composition: grid vs free-form, panel shapes, panel size, the 180-degree rule, rule of thirds, camera shots
  and angles, thumbnails to finished pages (p88-101).
- Ch5 inking (line weight: nearer and in-shadow lines heavier, thin broken lines recede; spotting blacks to
  separate planes), scanning, colour history, colour principles (warm advances, cool recedes; warm light / cool
  shadow), digital flats, lettering and balloons, logos (p106-145). Ch6 breaking in, glossary, index (p146-160).

## What this means for head_mhs3_n56 (q20)
- "Egyptian eyes" diagnosis: the eye is shaped like a front-view eye moved sideways. In 3/4 and profile the lid
  lines have to wrap the eyeball sphere: straight near the core, curving hard toward the horizon. The far corner
  is blunted and the near corner keeps its point. That is a per-view eye shape, not a scale. It confirms the plan
  to give the 3/4 and side views their own eye-shape keys rather than only stretching the front eye.
- Far eye placement: keep it shorter and behind the nose bridge wedge, never pushed out to the cheek edge.
- Profile check list: pupil centred in the iris, eye not set too deep, ear not dropped (p70).
- Lower face: smooth, subtle cheek-to-jaw contour on a young face (p70), agreeing with Hart and Character Designer.
