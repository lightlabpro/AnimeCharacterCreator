# Landscape Drawing Step by Step - Wendon Blake, drawings by Ferdinand Petrie (Dover, 64 PDF pages) - notes (q20)

Read: full text layer (all 64 PDF pages) + every page image (8 3x3 sheets). The scan is incomplete: printed page
numbers run to 80 and some pages are missing (e.g. pp.42-44, 48, 50-52, 64, 66, 68, 70-71, 74, 77, 79-80); this
is the file as provided.

## Core content
- Warm-ups: contour, groping line, scribbling, gesture drawing (p8-9). Measuring with pencil at arm's length -
  height, internal dimensions, width, slope; fitting shapes into imaginary boxes (p10-11).
- Linear perspective (even on curving streams, waves, clouds that flatten toward the horizon) and aerial
  perspective - nearer is darker, more contrasted, more detailed (p12-13).
- Values: a 10-step scale; three- and four-value systems; high vs low contrast; high vs low key (p14-17).
- Basic forms: cubes in light and shade (top plane lightest, one side halftone, other side shadow, cast shadow
  darkest next to the object); oblongs; cylinders and cones with the gradation light -> halftone -> shadow ->
  reflected light inside the shadow; spheres the same; irregular forms (snow, surf, clouds) still have distinct
  planes of light and shadow and "are just as solid" (p18-33).
- Pencil, chalk and charcoal stroke types and blending; ten demonstrations (trees, meadow, stream, rocky shore,
  mountains, surf, snow and ice, forest, pond, dunes) (p34-63).

## What this means for head_mhs3_n56 (q20)
- The sequence light -> halftone -> shadow -> reflected light on any rounded form (p22, p26) is the same reading
  How to Render gives; for our cel shading it supports one clear shadow shape per form (mouth interior, upper lip)
  rather than a flat colour patch, and keeping the darkest dark right where the forms meet (cast shadow darkest
  next to the object, p18) - i.e. at the top of the mouth opening under the upper lip.
