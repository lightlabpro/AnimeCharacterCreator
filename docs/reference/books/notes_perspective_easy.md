# Perspective Made Easy - Ernest R. Norling (Dover 1999 / Macmillan 1939, 228 PDF pages) - reading notes (q20)

Read: full text layer (every page, 2 chunks) + every page image (7 combined sheets). Pure perspective; no
anatomy. Page numbers below are PDF pages (book page numbers differ by about 18).

## Core content
- Horizon = eye level, always a straight line; everything above it is seen from below, everything below from
  above ("high-water mark", p21-37).
- Parallel lines converge to one vanishing point on the eye level; lines parallel to the picture plane do not
  converge (p41-48). Picture plane = a pane of glass between eye and object (p46-48).
- Two vanishing points; vertical lines stay vertical (p57-64). Place the two points far apart: close points give
  "violent" or "warped" perspective, i.e. the square object reads as a diamond (p67-74). One point moves slowly,
  the other quickly as the object turns; they always stay on opposite sides of the centre of interest (p77-83).
- Bricks build everything; man-high wall for placing figures (p87-106); avoid crowding overlapping objects
  (p105-106). The eye sees only a small area sharply; including too wide a field distorts the edges - two points
  cannot make a panorama (p109-114).
- Diagonals find perspective centres (p125-133). Circles in perspective are ellipses; the long axis of the
  ellipse is always at right angles to the cylinder's axis (the "T" rule), whatever the tilt (p137-160).
- Dividing surfaces, checkerboards (p161-172).
- Shade and shadow (p175-183): shade = surfaces facing away from the light; shadows follow perspective; "Shade on
  curved surfaces is usually darkest along the edge nearest the source of light" because reflected light
  brightens the far part of the shade side (luminous shadow, p181).
- Reflections (p187-191), up/down points (p195-199), uphill/downhill false eye level (p203-207), mechanical
  perspective (p211-221).

## What this means for head_mhs3_n56 (q20)
- The "Egyptian, elongated eyes" in 3/4 and side views can be made worse by the camera: a short focal length
  is the photographic version of vanishing points spaced too closely (p67-74) and stretches forms near the edge
  of the frame (p109-114). Board renders should use a long lens (orthographic or about 85mm+ equivalent) so the
  eye shape is judged without lens distortion. Check the camera before changing the mesh.
- Each eye's iris and its lid opening wrap a sphere: the iris ellipse's long axis stays perpendicular to the
  eye's gaze axis (p142-145). A turned eye narrows along that axis; it should not stretch sideways.
- The core shadow edge nearest the light is the darkest part of a shadow on a curved form (p181) - useful for
  the upper-lip and under-lip shadow bands in the toon ramp.
