# How to Draw and Paint Fantasy Architecture - Rob Alexander (Barron's 2011, 130 PDF pages) - reading notes (q20)

Read: full text layer (every page, 4 chunks; p8 of the text layer is a merged duplicate of later pages and was
checked against the images) + every page image (4 combined sheets). Architecture and painting; no figures
beyond small staffage. Page numbers are PDF pages.

## Core content
- Ch.1 styles (p10-52): Middle Eastern (domes, minarets, pendentives, arabesque), Romanesque (round arch, thick
  walls, horizontal emphasis), Gothic (pointed arch, buttresses, ribbed vaults, verticality), Mesoamerican
  (talud-tablero pyramids, post and lintel), Viking (longhouse, stave church), Asian (dominant roofs, horizontal
  axis, nature integration), modern/futurist. Repeated advice: a few recognisable features carry a style; form
  follows function.
- Ch.2 picture making (p54-74):
  - Light, shadow, value (p55-58): "The center of interest in your image will always be in the light";
    highlights where a plane changes direction; in a light area let either value or color dominate, not both;
    shadows thin, simple, transparent, with little detail - the eye flows past them; shadows usually warm
    under cool light; closer objects have more contrast, color, sharpness; limit the number of values (3-5) and
    don't change them once set; dark accents in shadows sparingly; one dominant light ("only one king").
  - Light types: volumetric, backlight, top light, classical 3/4 light from over the viewer's shoulder (p58).
  - Perspective, atmospheric perspective, spacing columns by diagonals (p59-62).
  - Concept then composition: thumbnails, simplify into masses, work the whole image at once, tension through
    contrast (p63-66).
  - Color (p67-70): color is relational; modify a color with its complement rather than black/gray; keep values
    when adding color; one color family dominates; the light-source color unifies the image; build shadows
    first, then local color, then lights.
  - Mood (p71-74): simplify when drama is lost; edit, don't copy.
- Ch.3 textures (p76-100): wood, joinery, stone, brick, thatch ("paint an impression... the viewer will extend
  the pattern", p91), plaster, marble (light enters several mm before scattering - translucent, p93),
  weathering, mosaics, fire and smoke. "A brushstroke can be used to convey color or value, but not both at
  once" (p84). Cast shadows warm at their edges (p94).
- Ch.4 artist demonstrations (p102-125): layering values for depth, mood through color, painting light,
  exploring ideas, establishing depth (massed shadows need not be very dark, just unified, p120), drama.

## What this means for head_mhs3_n56 (q20)
- No facial anatomy. Transferable to the toon shader and boards: keep shadow shapes flat, simple and few
  (upper-lip, mouth-interior, under-nose), give the shadow band a warm hue shift under cool light rather than
  plain gray, keep the focal light/contrast at the eyes, and limit the face to two or three value steps.
- Thatch/hair advice (p91): render masses with selective strands; the viewer extends the pattern.
- Marble translucency (p93) is the painter's version of subsurface scattering - relevant if skin ever moves off
  pure toon.
