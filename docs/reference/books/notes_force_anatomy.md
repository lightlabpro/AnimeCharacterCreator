# FORCE: Drawing Human Anatomy - Michael Mattesi (333 pp) - reading notes (q19)

Read: full text layer (every page) + every page image (3x3 sheets, 37 sheets). Page numbers = PDF pages.

## Method chapters (p21-84)
- The FORCE line: tapered at both ends, thickest at its apex; directional force + applied force = rhythm; balance
  across a centre line; avoid symmetric "dead" shapes (egg, ellipse, sausage, pipe, hourglass); use a
  straight-against-curve "FORCE shape" (p23-73).
- Form: surface lines that wrap the form; natural centres (sternum, navel, kneecap) and turning edges (p43-56).
- Skull = sphere + box; in steep angles find one clear angle (across the eyes) and run the nose, chin and forehead
  parallel to it (p54-55). Skeleton, joints, ligaments, tendons, spine S-curve, three rigid masses (p75-84).
- No fixed proportion system on purpose: each person differs (p89).

## Head, neck, upper back - front (p91-106)
- Facial muscle chart (p92): occipitofrontalis, procerus, depressor supercilii, orbicularis oculi, levator labii
  superioris, zygomaticus major/minor, risorius, depressor anguli oris, mentalis, depressor labii inferioris.
- Sternocleidomastoid: two bands from mastoid to sternum and clavicle forming a V/triangle at the throat; they turn
  and tilt the head (p93-96). Avoid the symmetric double-concave neck (p95).
- Jaw: simple forms; always keep in mind the LOWER PLANE of the jaw (p97) - the underside of the jaw is a separate
  plane (in shadow).
- Head as a cube with a turning edge; the front plane edges match; centre line of the face (p98).
- p99 figure: eyes at 1/2 of the head, nose at 1/2 of the lower half, mouth at 1/2 below that ("1/2 eyes, 1/2 nose,
  1/2 mouth"); eye, nose and mouth lines stay parallel in perspective and may converge toward the back.
- Eye, nostril and mouth lines parallel (p100); neck = straight-to-curve shape.
- Eye mask and mouth mask simplify expression (p101); opposing forces in the mouth, rhythm in the brows (p102).
- Eyes: the lids wrap the eyeball sphere; the peaks (apexes) of the upper and lower lid are OFFSET, not aligned
  (aligned = static) (p103); the lid has thickness between lash and eyeball; the iris seen at an angle is an
  ASYMMETRIC ellipse (one side flatter) (p104).
- Nose: four planes; a triangle for the front turning edge and the bottom plane (p105).
- MOUTH: "it wraps around the curvature of the teeth"; a centre line divides the lips into quadrants and defines
  their top and bottom surfaces (p105). -> the upper lip has a downward-facing/receding upper surface and the lip
  line curves back with the dental arch; at 3/4 the far half of the mouth turns away and shortens.

## Head, neck - side (p107-117)
- Upper back cantilevers the head; force runs up the back of the neck and out through the face (p107-112).
- Profile contour = alternating convex and concave curves with crisp "edges" where they flip; smoothing these out
  is the typical mistake (p113-114). Nose profile (convex, concave, straight) sets character (p113).
- Brow, nose and jaw edges are parallel in a profile (p115).
- EAR (p116): a "Y" shape inside (helix/antihelix branches); fit the flat ear in a rectangle to read its proportion;
  wide-to-narrow arcs; the ear has thickness - narrow width in perspective.

## Rest of the body (p119-324)
- Trapezius diamond (p119-129); upper girdle, pecs ("football pads"), deltoid teardrop centred on the arm (p131-152);
  scapula family (p153-162); arm, forearm zones, hands ("mitten", butter-knife fingers) (p163-205); abs, obliques,
  serratus, lats (p207-232); pelvis "basin", male vs female, wrench shape, glutes, TFL (p233-260); thighs, sartorius,
  "outside-inside-outside" rhythm (p261-293); knees, calves, feet (p295-324). Read in full; body-only content.

## For our head
- Upper lip: model/paint it as a plane that turns down and back under the nose (the philtrum area above it faces
  forward, the lip's top surface faces down) -> a thin shade on the upper lip, lit lower lip top, shade under it.
- Mouth follows the dental arch: at 3/4 the far half of the mouth recedes (shorter), the near half is longer.
- Jaw underside is its own plane: shade it; the chin/jaw contour flips convex/concave with crisp edges.
- Eyes: lid apexes offset; at an angle the iris becomes an asymmetric ellipse, not a stretched almond.
