# Building Reference-Free Anatomy Validators for Anime Characters

The project can automate most of `docs/anatomy-manual.md` without a per-character reference model, but only by splitting the job into three tiers of different trustworthiness. **Tier 1 is exact rules** (limb and finger counts, bone chain order, weight health, left/right symmetry at rest, features inside the head). These need no corpus and can fail a pack today. **Tier 2 is ratio checks** (7 to 7.5 heads, pubis at midpoint, wrists at crotch, thigh about equal to lower leg, loop counts) that compare scale-free measurements against the manual's own numbers, with per-style bands. **Tier 3 is relative pose tests** (posed versus rest, left versus right, slider extreme versus slider zero) that need no reference mesh because the mesh is compared with itself. The research found no published, standard QA suite for any of these. Landmark extraction, volume and stretch formulas, quad-quality ranges and robust statistics are well documented, but every pass/fail threshold specific to skinning, topology, hands or anime faces is an engineering guess that has to be calibrated on this project's own good and bad packs. The most important caveat is evidentiary: many primary sources (Blender docs, Khronos docs, PubMed Central, ANSUR tables, Disney and Sandia pages) were blocked, so most "confirmed" items below rest on search summaries. The build order that follows from this is hard rules first, then rest-pose proportions and symmetry, then a pose-stress harness, then topology loop counting, with learned or VLM review last and advisory only.

## What is confirmed, what is inferred, and what is missing

The research notes sort cleanly into three evidence grades, and the report treats them differently. The **confirmed** layer is mostly mechanics and conventions. glTF stores skinning as `JOINTS_0` and `WEIGHTS_0`, four influences per attribute set, with extra sets for more ([Khronos glTF tutorial](https://github.khronos.org/glTF-Tutorials/gltfTutorial/gltfTutorial_020_Skins.html)). Linear blend skinning collapses at twisted joints (the candy-wrapper effect), while dual quaternion skinning keeps volume but bulges at bent joints and flips ([Kavan et al.](https://users.cs.utah.edu/~ladislav/dq/)). Blender ships weight repair tools (Normalize All, Clean, Limit Total) that correspond to the checks one would write ([Blender manual](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/4.3/sculpt_paint/weight_paint/editing.html)). `mathutils.BVHTree.overlap()` supports self-intersection by skipping triangle pairs that share two or more vertices ([Blender commit 231ee60](https://projects.blender.org/archive/blender-archive/commit/231ee60ab5373bb2e5676fc4f5c6dea476fc1d42)). Measurement tools define landmarks as fixed points plus point-to-point distances, and circumferences as plane cuts, in a neutral pose ([SMPL-Anthropometry](https://github.com/DavidBoja/SMPL-Anthropometry), [3d-body-measurements](https://github.com/vcarlosrb/3d-body-measurements)). VRM 1.0 fixes bone hierarchy and naming, with finger bones optional, but enforces no proportions ([VRM humanoid spec](https://github.com/vrm-c/vrm-specification/blob/master/specification/VRMC_vrm-1.0/humanoid.md)). Quad quality has published acceptable ranges from the Cubit/Verdict documentation: aspect ratio 1 to 4, skew 0 to 0.5, scaled Jacobian 0.5 to 1 ([Cubit quad metrics](https://www.sandia.gov/files/cubit/15.3/help_manual/WebHelp/mesh_generation/mesh_quality_assessment/quadrilateral_metrics.htm)), though those target finite-element solvers, not skinning.

The **inferred** layer is where almost all the pass/fail logic lives. The detector table for skinning (unweighted vertices, weight islands, weight bleed between fingers, Laplacian smoothness outliers, ring-radius ratio under twist, volume ratio under bend, edge stretch, deformation-gradient determinant for flips, self-intersection against a rest baseline), the loop-counting algorithm, the pole and n-gon rules, the mirror-Chamfer symmetry score, and the finger chain-length ordering are all the research team's own designs. They are standard geometry and sensible, but nobody has published thresholds for them. Starting values quoted in the notes, such as a ring-radius ratio above 0.6 at 90 degrees of twist, a volume ratio between 0.75 and 1.15 at a 90 degree bend, an edge-stretch 99.9th percentile within 0.5 to 2.0, a mirror 95th percentile under 0.5% of height, and a 2% L/R pose asymmetry, are explicitly guesses. The "at least 3 loops per joint" rule is community guidance, attributed through a search summary to a CG Cookie thread ([CG Cookie](https://cgcookie.com/community/9812-searching-for-topology-and-modeling-advice)). Notably, the project manual's own loop counts (shoulder 3 to 5, elbow 2 to 4, knee 4 to 6, face 8 to 12) are the only numeric loop table available, and they are more specific than anything the research retrieved.

The **gaps** matter because they bound what can be promised. No source gave anime body-proportion statistics beyond tutorial-grade head-count ranges (realistic 7 to 8 heads, anime 6 to 9 or more, chibi 2 to 4, from aggregators such as [Clip Studio Tips](https://tips.clip-studio.com/en-us/articles/4888)). No measured hand-to-face or foot-to-forearm rule was found, so the manual's "hand about face length" cannot be justified from literature and has to be calibrated on this project's references. Studio range-of-motion pass criteria were not found, and the Rigify, Auto-Rig Pro and Mixamo weight-report features were not checked. Current bpy 5.x signatures were not verified, and performance numbers are estimates. The AAOS joint ranges used for the stress poses (shoulder flexion 180 degrees, elbow 150, hip 120, knee 135) come from secondary clinical sources, and the neck figure in them conflicts with other references ([Enlyte](https://www.enlyte.com/insights/article/adjuster/understanding-normal-range-motion-joint-functionality)). The calibration-statistics advice (modified z-score cutoff of 3.5, conformal quantiles) is sourced only from secondary summaries ([MetricGate](https://metricgate.com/calculator/robust-z-score-modified), [conformal prediction tutorial](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.07511)), and no published sample-size guidance for anatomy-ratio bands exists. The earlier AI-pipeline research reaches the same structural conclusion from the generation side: the field validates with several independent signals, and no source covers closed-loop validators for anime rigs, so thresholds must come from local labelled data.

## Landmarks come from the rig first, the mesh second

Every proportion rule needs landmarks, and the research converges on one approach that suits this stack: derive them deterministically from DEF- bones and vertex weights, not from detectors. The inferred recipe measures in rest pose (T or A) with a stature taken from the mesh, not the armature. Top of head is the maximum Y of head-weighted vertices, sole is the minimum Y, and joint heights come from bone head positions (`upper_arm` head for the shoulder, `lower_leg` head for the knee, `hand` head for the wrist). Crotch height comes from the lowest slice of vertices weighted to both legs, and widths come from silhouette slices at bone heights on the nude base body. The notes caution that the distance between upper-arm heads understates bideltoid breadth, so shoulder width must be a mesh slice. Head height is chin to vertex of the head mesh, and head count is stature divided by that. This is the manual's metric, so the validator can express the manual's rules directly: pubis at the midpoint is crotch height over stature near 0.5, wrists at the crotch is wrist-bone height against crotch height, thigh against lower leg is the length ratio of the two bone segments or of the mesh spans they drive, shoulders near 2 heads and hips near 1.5 are slice widths divided by head height, and hand near face length is hand-vertex extent divided by head-vertex height of the face.

Where the rig cannot supply a landmark, the design must return exit 13, not a guess. Pubis and chin are the likely weak points, because DEF- bones sit at joints and not at surface features. The cleanest fix is a small, optional set of landmark empties exported with the pack, alongside the existing SOC- sockets (for example one each for pubis, chin, brow line, nose base and mouth centre). That is a contract change under the project's own rule, so it needs an inbox item to the chat first. Until it lands, the validator can approximate pubis from the crotch slice and chin from the lowest head-weighted front-half vertex, and mark those results as lower-confidence warnings, never fails. Note the nuance that glTF loaders rename nodes (`DEF-upper_arm.L` becomes `DEF-upper_armL`) so any new code that matches bones must use the authored-name rule already in `gltfPacks.ts`, and Python-side readers of the raw glTF should match `.L`/`.R` names directly.

For real-human context, ANSUR-derived ratios give a sanity anchor for the realistic profile only. Computed from search-summary values (not verified at source), sitting height is about **0.52 of stature** in both sexes, biacromial breadth about 0.237 (male) and 0.224 (female) of stature, and hip breadth about 0.196 and 0.217; shoulder-to-hip ratio runs around 1.27 to 1.30 for men and 1.14 to 1.16 for women ([NCSU ergocenter table](https://www.ergocenter.ncsu.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/18/2017/09/Anthropometric-Summary-Data-Tables.pdf), [Hughes 2003](https://www.psy.uq.edu.au/~uqbziets/Hughes2003%20-%20Shoulder%20to%20hip%20ratio.pdf)). These are useful for the sex-variant check (fail only if the ordering is inverted against the declared sex), but they describe real adults, so the manual's targets, not these, must set the anime bands. Likewise, the SMPL shape space is non-commercial and does not model chibi or heroic anime, so it is at most a realistic-profile reference ([SMPL license](https://smpl.is.tue.mpg.de/license.html)).

## Pose, skinning and symmetry tests that need no reference mesh

The strongest idea in the notes is that deformation quality can be judged relatively, which removes the need for a reference per character. Every deformation metric is a ratio of posed to rest: ring radius at mid-limb under twist (the cheap candy-wrapper detector), regional volume under a bend, per-edge stretch, the sign of the per-triangle deformation-gradient determinant for flips, and new self-intersections after subtracting overlaps already present at rest (eyes in head, layered clothing, teeth). Left/right comparison supplies a second reference: apply the mirrored pose to the mirrored bone, mirror the posed vertices through a rest-pose correspondence map, and compare. Any asymmetry already present at rest is subtracted so asymmetric characters do not fail. A third reference is the slider sweep: run the same pose set at slider zero and at each shape-key extreme, and require that metrics do not degrade by more than a margin, which finds shape keys that make hips collide or limbs thin to nothing. glTF applies morph targets before skinning, so the sweep can run on the exported pack in numpy. The notes also propose comparing LBS against dual quaternion volume: if only LBS loses volume the cause is the skinning method, while if both lose volume the weights or joint placement are at fault. That separation is inferred reasoning and untested, but it tells an agent which fix to try.

Static weight checks need no pose at all and are the most dependable part of this tier: zero-sum or unweighted vertices, a weight-sum error above 1e-3, more than four influences (the glTF attribute-set limit the app uses), a high fraction of tiny weights, disconnected weight islands per bone (run per mesh island, because eyes and accessories are separate by design), and finger-to-finger bleed that causes fused fingers. Weight smoothness is a reference-free property in the literature ([Solomon quasi-harmonic skinning](https://people.csail.mit.edu/jsolomon/assets/quasiharmonic.pdf)), so a Laplacian energy outlier rule (median plus several MAD, plus an absolute floor) is defensible. Hands add exact skeleton rules from VRM naming: four fingers with proximal, intermediate and distal bones, a thumb with metacarpal, proximal and distal, middle finger longest and thumb shortest, and chain length computed along the actual node path because non-humanoid nodes may sit between humanoid bones ([VRM humanoid spec](https://github.com/vrm-c/vrm-specification/blob/master/specification/VRMC_vrm-1.0/humanoid.md)). Common rig failures named by practitioner sources (fused fingers, collapsing joints, candy-wrapper twist, stray distant-bone weights) map directly onto these checks ([seeles.ai rigging guide](https://www.seeles.ai/resources/blogs/3d-model-rigging-guide)). The stress-pose library (upper-arm twist at plus and minus 90 and 150 degrees, elbow 90/135/150, knee 90/135/150, hip 90/120, finger curls of about 90/100/70) is inferred from clinical ranges and should be tightened or loosened per rig convention; stylised proportions may need looser bounds.

Symmetry is the easiest reference-free score. Reflect vertices through the expected x=0 plane, take nearest-neighbour distances, and report mean, 95th percentile and max normalised by height, gated on body and face meshes only because hair and accessories are asymmetric by design. Proposed floors (p95 under 0.5% of height, max under 2%) are guesses. A second, index-level symmetry check pairs each left vertex with a mirror partner and requires one-to-one matching, mirrored shape-key deltas and equal weights on `.L` and `.R` bones, which protects the sliders and performance keys. The registration literature supports finding the plane by ICP when x=0 fails ([Finding Mirror Symmetry via Registration](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1611.05971)), but for authored Blender characters the plane is known, so this is a fallback only. Blender's topology mirror, which pairs vertices by connectivity, is useful for the index check but is documented as unreliable on simple primitives ([Blender wiki](https://wiki.blender.org/index.php/User:Terrywallwork/WorkingOn/Topology_Mirror)).

## Topology, face placement and variants need Blender and honest bands

Edge flow cannot be fully checked from the exported glTF, because glTF is triangulated; true quad ratio, n-gon locations and authored loop walking need bmesh in headless Blender (a numpy path can only report a quad-recoverable ratio by pairing coplanar triangles). Professional guidance is qualitative: loops encircle every joint and expression area, denser where deformation is greater, quads in deforming zones, poles kept away from high-deformation areas, and tighter rings on the crease side of elbows and knees ([NovEdge ZBrush tip](https://novedge.com/blogs/design-news/zbrush-tip-deformation-ready-joint-topology-with-zremesher), search summary only). The proposed computation slices the mesh by planes perpendicular to each joint bone across the region it controls, counts distinct closed rings, and compares with the manual's ranges (shoulder 3 to 5, elbow 2 to 4, knee 4 to 6). Valence flags poles of 3 or 5 or more edges inside regions weighted above about 0.5 to a joint bone, and n-gons fail in deforming zones. Face loops are counted as closed rings by winding number around the eye and mouth landmarks, with the manual's 8 to 12 face loops as the target. The notes found no sourced loop numbers for hips, wrists, or low-poly anime norms (1 to 2 loops per joint is plausible but unconfirmed), and no mainstream validator checks edge flow at all: VRChat ranks by counts, and UniVRM checks hierarchy and facing ([VRChat wiki](https://wiki.vrchat.com/wiki/Avatar_Performance_Ranking), [UniVRM export dialog](https://vrm.dev/en/univrm/export/univrm_export/)). This is a genuine opening, but it also means no external calibration exists. Expect more false positives here than anywhere else, because deliberate low-poly choices can look identical to mistakes; this check should warn, not fail, until calibrated.

Face placement should be measured on the mesh, not on renders. Anime face detectors exist but are limited to near-frontal views (the hysts detector predicts 28 landmarks, [GitHub](https://github.com/hysts/anime-face-detector)), and photo-trained tools such as MediaPipe have no documented accuracy on stylised faces; both are untested here. The mesh route uses eye and jaw bones, vertex groups, and shape-key delta regions (mouth-open, blink) to locate centroids deterministically at any pose. The classical canons (face in fifths, intercanthal distance equal to nasal width, thirds) are poorly followed even in real populations, with the nose wider than the eye gap in 92% of men in one cohort ([Al-Sebaei 2015](https://head-face-med.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s13005-015-0064-y)), and anime eyes are far larger than real ones (eye height 30 to 90% of nose height against 15 to 33% for humans, a moderate-confidence summary from [core.ac.uk](https://api.core.ac.uk/oai/oai:localhost:310904400/112410)). So the check should be gross-violation only: feature order from top to bottom (brow, eye, nose, mouth, chin), eye line at roughly 0.45 to 0.60 of head height (inferred band), all landmarks inside the face silhouette, left/right symmetry about the face midline, eyeballs inside sockets with lids covering them at blink=1, and teeth and tongue staying inside the head at maximum viseme. These extend `head-shape-audit` and `knowledge/head-targets.json`, which already pin head silhouette proportions.

Age, sex and style variants should be handled by requiring a declared style profile (realistic, anime adult, teen, child, chibi) and showing it in every report, with a warning if a head-count classifier disagrees; silent auto-guessing would let a wrong profile hide a real defect. The notes' proposed head-count bands are a synthesis, not measurements: realistic adult 7.0 to 8.0, anime adult 6.5 to 8.5, teen 6 to 7.5, child 4.5 to 6, chibi 2 to 4. For this project the better source is the project's own manual and the MHS3 reference, so the first profile should be the manual's numbers with a tolerance of about 0.5 head and 10 to 20% on other ratios (placeholders). Sex checks need only ordering (male shoulder-to-hip above female for the same style) with wide margins. Child and age curves are the weakest area: head-fraction figures run from about a quarter of length at birth to an eighth for adults, but the retrieved sources conflict at age 10 and offer no ratios for sitting height or leg length at specific ages ([Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/human-body-ratios)), so age variants should return unknown until measured from the project's own variants.

## A prioritised build plan mapped to the existing stack

All new validators should follow the repo's conventions: tri-state exit codes (0 pass, 12 failed, 13 unknown, 2 usage), one verdict per region with the worst severity winning (a good body must never mask a bad hand), and findings that name a region, the landmark pair, the measured value, the expected band and a one-sentence fix. The notes' severity scheme (error, warn, info, as in glTF-Validator, [issue #14](https://github.com/khronosgroup/gltf-validator/issues/14)) fits: only hard rules and wide-band checks may produce 12, and narrower bands produce warnings. Unknown is returned when a landmark or bone is missing, when the style has too few calibration samples, or when a required view or reviewer is unavailable, and never counts as pass.

| Priority | Validator (home) | Inputs | Reports | Calibration | Cannot catch |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `anatomy_rules.py` hard rules, added to `library-pack-check` (numpy on glTF) | DEF- bone names and hierarchy, `JOINTS_0`/`WEIGHTS_0`, rest-pose vertices | 12 on wrong finger or limb count, broken chain order, unweighted vertex, weight sum off by over 1e-3, more than 4 influences, finger bleed, L/R index asymmetry; 13 if rig missing | Good pack passes; make 3 to 5 deliberately broken packs (swap forearm weights, un-normalise, delete a finger) and confirm each fails; pin in a fixture test | Bad art that is structurally valid (a perfectly rigged but ugly hand) |
| 2 | `proportion_audit.py` extending `head-shape-audit` (Blender or glTF, rest pose) | Bone heads, head-weighted vertices, slices at bone heights; optional landmark empties (needs inbox item) | Warn/fail per manual ratio against the declared style profile; 13 if a landmark is missing | Start from the manual's numbers; measure the existing MHS3 and good packs, set warn at observed spread, fail at a wide margin; held-out good packs measure the false-alarm rate | Wrong proportions that are internally consistent and match a wrong declared style; surface details |
| 3 | `symmetry_audit.py` (numpy, plus Blender for index pairing) | Body and face meshes, expected x=0 plane | Chamfer mean, p95, max normalised by height; fail only past a wide floor | Measure real packs; guesses are p95 under 0.5%, max under 2%; subtract rest asymmetry | Intentional asymmetry hidden in flagged-tolerant zones; small asymmetric sculpting under the floor |
| 4 | `pose_stress.py` harness (numpy LBS on glTF; Blender BVH for self-intersection) | Armature, weights, pose library, slider keys | Ratios per joint region and pose at 50/100/120% amplitude; self-intersection counts versus rest; slider-extreme degradation; 12 on flips, stretch above 3, volume below 0.6; 13 if no weights or open mesh for volume | Run on one good and several broken rigs; place each floor between them; record in the validator calibration file | Cloth, hair and secondary motion; dynamics; stylised joints that legitimately collapse |
| 5 | `topology_audit.py` (bmesh in headless Blender, covered by `tests/blender/`) | Authored mesh with quads, joint bones, eye and mouth landmarks | Loop counts versus the manual's ranges, poles and n-gons in deforming zones, quad quality; warn only at first | Count loops on the existing assets and the manual targets; promote to fail only for n-gons and zero-area faces | Whether the flow is aesthetically right; deliberate low-poly joints |
| 6 | Face-placement extension of `head_audit.py` | Eye and jaw bones, vertex groups, shape-key delta regions | Feature order, eye-line band, inside-silhouette, blink and viseme containment; 13 when regions missing | Bands from `knowledge/head-targets.json` and the MHS3 reference; widen for anime eyes | Expression quality, likeness, eye-contact feel |
| 7 | Corpus ratchet and optional VLM pass (render-validator) | Ratio vectors from licence-clean or author-owned models; fixed-view crops | Held-out false-alarm rate in CI; advisory VLM findings; reviewer disagreement gives 13 | Robust median and MAD per style with at least 30 to 50 per style (rule of thumb); fewer than about 20 gives unknown | Anything outside the corpus style; the VLM can raise a fail but never clear a metric failure |

The first priority is the cheapest and most valuable because it needs no calibration beyond a few broken fixtures and moves most failures from "the app silently misbehaves" to a named exit 12. It also fits the existing contract checker, which already inspects bones, sockets and shape-key names. Priority two uses the manual directly and is where the "without a reference per character" claim is delivered, since the target is the declared style profile and not a model. Priority four is the largest engineering item but the only one that catches the failures users actually see on a posed character. Run it with numpy skinning on the glTF for speed and use Blender only for the BVH overlap and for the dual-quaternion comparison, and measure runtime before promising it (the notes' minutes-scale estimate is unmeasured). Landmark empties and a stable, declared style profile are the two pack-contract additions that unlock the most checks, so raise those with the chat before building priority two.

Calibration should copy the project's existing approach: put every floor in a calibration file, pin it with a test, and place each threshold between known-good and deliberately broken cases. The notes add three refinements worth adopting. First, build per-style profiles using a median and MAD rule (warn near a modified z of 3.5, fail far beyond), keep the fail band wide and the warn band narrow, and split the calibration and held-out sets by author so near-duplicates do not straddle them. Second, add a ratchet: store results for known-good fixtures and fail CI if any flips or the held-out false-alarm rate rises. Third, because proxy scores climb while true quality stalls under optimisation pressure ([Gao et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.10760)), do not reveal thresholds to an iterating agent. Return the failing region, landmark and direction, round the values, and keep at least one independent hidden check, such as mesh-derived limb length alongside the bone-derived one. The corpus itself is limited by licensing: VRoid Hub's terms prohibit reverse engineering and each VRM sets its own redistribution rights ([VRoid Hub policy](https://hub.vroid.com/en/content-protection-policy)), and the known anime datasets (about 14k VRoid Hub models, filtered to 10,811 A-pose characters in [StdGEN](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.05738)) skew to young female A-pose avatars. The practical corpus is the project's own packs plus permitted models, storing only derived ratio vectors.

VLM review stays last and advisory. Learned scorers such as Hi3DEval and 3DGen-Bench were trained on generator outputs and human preference, not anatomical correctness ([Hi3DEval](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.05609v1), [3DGen-Bench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.21745)), and VLMs hallucinate finger parts and struggle with hand structure ([HandVQA summary](https://chatpaper.com/es/paper/291977)), so counting must be geometric. If used, the structure is fixed views, per-region crops, a closed yes/no/cannot-see checklist, the measured numbers as context, and two independent reviewers with randomised ordering because LLM judges show position bias ([position bias study](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.07791v7)); disagreement maps to exit 13.

## Conclusion

The research changes the framing of the question. "Smart enough to check anatomy without a reference" is not one capability but three with different evidence: exact structural rules that need no data, relative self-comparison tests that need no reference model, and ratio bands that need only a declared style and a small local calibration set. The project already holds the hardest ingredient, a numeric manual written by someone who knows what the target should be, which is why the plan can start from the manual and not from a corpus the literature cannot supply. What the validators will remain unable to judge is the part the literature also cannot: whether a stylised exaggeration is good art. They can say a forearm is 30% too short or an elbow has one loop, and they can prove the rig does not collapse, but they cannot say the character is appealing, so a human or independent review must remain the last gate.

The most useful next step is empirical, not more reading. Build priority one and priority two on the existing good pack, break it in five or six deliberate ways to see which checks fire, and record the resulting numbers in the calibration file. Those measurements will replace most of the guessed thresholds in this report, and they will also reveal whether landmark empties are truly needed or whether bones and weight extremes are enough.
